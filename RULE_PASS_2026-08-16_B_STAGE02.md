## B1 — One anchor card, two lives

**The two rows describing the card are ruled as ONE component.** The agent
already flagged the drift ("a second, smaller drawing of the catalogue card,
and the two will drift"). It is right, and the fix is not to reconcile two
drawings but to stop having two.

An anchor card has an **empty life** and a **filled life**, exactly like
PANEL_CARD (2026-08-03) — one component, two states, not the filled layout
minus its content:

**Empty life** (nothing chosen): head · the SETS / NOT line · one button.
No plate, no dashed well, nothing reserving the shape of the missing thing.
This is what shipped and it is correct.

**Filled life** (a style chosen): **the picture is the largest element on
the card**, at its own ratio, first — then the name, then the words, then a
Courier provenance line where one exists. Clicking anywhere on it reopens
the panel, because the thing you are looking at is the thing you would
change. For a cinematography grammar the picture is that grammar's **three
reference frames as a strip**; one plate cannot stand for three.

Both lives render from **one function** shared with the catalogue cell, at
two scales. If the catalogue card gains a field, the anchor card gains it or
deliberately drops it in one place.

The row's own worry — "a card that is nothing but a button, where the
SETS/NOT line has to carry the whole preview" — resolves itself: the card is
only nothing-but-a-button in the life where there is genuinely nothing to
show. That is not a preview problem, it is an honest empty state.

## B2 — Diagrams are a stated placeholder, never the answer

**The SVG plate is ALLOWED as a placeholder and REFUSED as the design.**

A diagram teaches an axis. A style picker has to sell a look. Those are not
the same job, and the row saying so is correct.

- Diagrams stay until real frames land, with the manifest slot they already
  have.
- The disclosure is **one line per panel, not per card**: a Courier line at
  the head of the catalogue reading `PLATES ARE DIAGRAMS UNTIL THE REFERENCE
  LIBRARY LANDS`. Nine marks on nine cards is noise; one statement is honest.
- **Cinematography is exempt and must not use diagrams** — its grammars
  already carry three real reference frames from `docs/CINEMATOGRAPHY_STYLES.md`.
  Use them.
- The row worrying that a real captured panel makes the eight diagrams look
  unfinished has the argument backwards. They ARE unfinished. That is
  evidence for finishing them, not for hiding the real one.

## B3 — Never pad to three

**The three empty dashed cells are REFUSED.**

Canon forbids reserving the shape of the missing thing. The one sanctioned
exception is stage 03's empty frame, which earns it by **stating the blocker
that keeps it empty** — a dashed cell that says nothing is not that.

A grammar with no reference frames shows **no cell area at all** and one
Courier line: `REFERENCE FRAMES — NOT YET IN THE LIBRARY`. A grammar with
one or two frames shows one or two. Never pad to three.

## B4 — Seven text roles is not a card, it is a page

**REFUSED. The grammar card cuts to four roles; the essay moves behind one door.**

Canon's cards carry two or three roles. Seven is a document rendered as a
cell, and it is why the description wrapped to a column an inch wide at
three-across.

**On the card:** the picture (three frames) · the name (15/600) · the
description (15/400 `--ink-dim`, capped 720px) · one line of reference films
· one link, `Read the grammar`.

**Behind the link:** the key question, the operating principle, and the full
image-model prompt, in one reading view with Copy. Three things that reward
reading are three things nobody reads inside a grid cell.

Two-across stands. Do not go back to three.

## B5 — No italic; a name a human wrote is Archivo

Two REFUSALS, both small, both canon:

- **Italic is refused outright.** The system has two voices and they are
  families, not slants. Adding italic for emphasis is how a third voice
  starts. Add to `## Do not`: *no italic — emphasis is ink tier, weight or
  position.* The key question keeps its emphasis by being first in the
  reading view.
- **The reference films leave Courier.** Courier is machine data — ids,
  counts, hashes, states. Film titles are proper nouns a human wrote, and
  setting them in Courier claims they were emitted by something. Archivo
  11.5px `--ink-dim`, comma-separated, wrapping. Canon, to `## Copy`:
  **a name a human wrote is Archivo, even in a list shaped like data.**

## B6 — The captured card says what kind of thing it is

**The capture is RATIFIED. Its invisibility is REFUSED.**

Capturing the production's own style from the bible's Required bullets and a
real approved panel is the best idea in this batch — a production that has
been rendering for weeks should not be offered a phrase we wrote.

But a generated entry and an authored entry that look identical are two
kinds of thing wearing one costume. The card carries a Courier provenance
line **as its first line**, not buried in its description:

`FROM THIS PRODUCTION — ITS BIBLE AND ITS APPROVED PANELS`

and a **hairline after it**, separating what this production made from what
we authored. Position alone does not disclose; a hairline plus a line of
provenance does. The shipped fallback for a production with no bible stays.

## B7 — An escape hatch is not the tenth cell of a catalogue

**REFUSED in both places it currently appears — and it currently appears twice.**

`Add your own` sits as the last cell of the grid, and the picker modal
carries a free-text escape hatch of its own. That is one control drawn two
ways, which `one-control-two-presentations` (canon pass 2026-08-10) already
forbids.

Ship **one**: below the grid, after a hairline, full width, as a labelled
row carrying the `+` for an image and the field for words. Not a cell — a
set-member tile classifies by fill (§1.3), and an escape hatch is not a
member of the set it escapes. This also answers the picker row's question
about where the free-text belongs: same place, same control, once.

## B8 — The definition-by-negation is canon

**RATIFIED and promoted.** Goes to `## Copy`.

> **An axis the app has never taught is stated by what it is NOT, before its
> options appear.** `not mood, not light, not cinematography` does more work
> than a paragraph of definition, and it is the first place this app teaches
> rather than assumes.

The trigger showing the chosen style's NAME rather than the directive it
writes is correct — a field whose answer comes from a known vocabulary is a
choice, and a choice shows what was chosen. Selection as an ink keyline with
amber reserved for `Use this` is correct and needs no change.

The catalogues' CONTENT — nine rendering styles, nine cinematography looks —
is a director's call, not a designer's. Flagged to the user, not ruled here.

## B10 — The experiment, and the provenance it owes

**The lock strip is RATIFIED** — stated state, one act, OFF by default,
byte-identical renders until thrown, asymmetric confirm (see **A4**).

**Placement is RATIFIED:** the experiment stays in the panel that sets the
thing. `verb-sits-with-its-object` (canonized 2026-08-07) governs — a
switch that changes what a grammar does belongs with the grammar, not in a
settings surface that knows nothing about this production.

**The missing provenance is a DEFECT and blocks this row's canonization.**
A take records whether the grammar rode it and no screen says so. Canon:
*facts about an image ride on it.* The take's fact line gains, in Courier,
on any take that rode:

`GRAMMAR — <NAME>`

on the hero and in the lightbox; absent entirely on takes that did not ride.
Without this the experiment cannot be evaluated, which is the whole reason
the switch was built.
