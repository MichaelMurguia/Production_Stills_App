# CINEMATIC LENS, FRAMING & CAMERA RECIPES

This document treats camera specifications as **cinematic language**, not metadata. The useful question is not merely “what lens?” but **what combination of lens, camera position, focus, aperture and movement creates the desired relationship between subject, environment and audience?**

**Format assumption:** focal lengths below use a **full-frame / 35mm-equivalent field of view** for easy comparison. If the actual capture format changes, convert focal length to preserve the intended field of view.

---

# 1. Lens Parameters — What They Affect

| Parameter | Practical choices | What changes in the image | Cinematic consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Focal length** | 14–20mm ultra-wide | Extremely broad field of view; strong edge stretching; foreground becomes very large when camera is close | Visceral, aggressive, huge near-to-far depth, dramatic movement toward camera |
|  | 21–28mm wide | Broad field of view; pronounced foreground scale; strong spatial separation | Immersion, action geography, environmental storytelling, strong FG/MG/BG |
|  | 32–40mm moderate wide | Wide enough for environment without conspicuous distortion | Classic narrative balance of character and environment |
|  | 45–55mm normal | Neutral field of view; less obvious optical signature | Observational, balanced, human-scale |
|  | 65–100mm short telephoto | Narrower field; usually requires greater camera distance for same framing | Character isolation, cleaner backgrounds, intimate or controlled compositions |
|  | 100–200mm telephoto | Narrow field; same framing requires distant camera | Apparent spatial compression, stacked subjects, detached observation |
|  | 200mm+ long telephoto | Very narrow field and distant camera position | Surveillance, isolation, graphic compression, remote point of view |
| **Aperture / f-stop** | f/1.4–2 | Very shallow depth of field | One plane dominates; subjective, intimate, selective |
|  | f/2.8–4 | Moderate subject separation | Character priority while environment remains legible |
|  | f/5.6–8 | Medium/deep depth | Strong narrative readability and ensemble staging |
|  | f/11–16 | Deep focus | Multiple planes remain simultaneously important; architecture and mise-en-scène dominate |
| **Focus distance** | Near / subject / middle distance / far | Determines the plane of maximum sharpness | Tells the audience **where attention lives** in the physical space |
| **Depth of field** | Shallow / medium / deep | Controls how much space around the focus plane remains acceptably sharp | Changes the frame from selective attention to relational spatial storytelling |
| **Camera-to-subject distance** | Close / moderate / distant | **Actually determines perspective** and relative near/far scale | Close = immersive and dimensional; distant = flatter and more observational |
| **Lens distortion** | Corrected / moderate wide-angle / strong wide-angle | Changes straight-line behavior and edge stretching | Can preserve architectural precision or make proximity feel visceral |
| **Lens type** | Spherical | Conventional geometry and bokeh | Neutral-to-characterful depending on lens family |
|  | Anamorphic | Different horizontal field, oval bokeh, edge behavior and flare characteristics | Expansive horizontal staging and a recognizable optical character |
| **Optical character** | Modern / vintage / low-contrast / high-contrast | Changes flare, aberration, sharpness falloff and contrast | Clinical precision vs romantic, period or imperfect optical character |
| **Shutter angle** | 45–90° | Less motion blur | Crisp, staccato, violent or hyper-alert movement |
|  | 180° | Familiar motion blur | Conventional cinematic movement |
|  | 270–360° | Increased motion smear | Frenetic, dreamlike or disorienting movement |
| **ISO / sensitivity** | Low | Cleaner image, more light required | Controlled, polished capture |
|  | High | Allows lower-light exposure; potentially more noise and reduced latitude | Available-light immediacy or rougher photographic texture |
| **Capture format** | S16 / S35 / full frame / 65mm | Changes field of view for a given focal length and depth-of-field behavior for equivalent framing | Must be known before a focal-length number has precise physical meaning |

## Critical rule: focal length does not determine perspective by itself

**Camera position determines perspective. Focal length determines field of view from that position.**

A close 24mm and a distant 100mm can frame a person at approximately the same size, but the images are radically different. The close camera exaggerates near/far scale; the distant camera reduces those differences and creates the familiar appearance of telephoto compression.

# 2. Desired Framing / Look — Camera Recipes

| Desired framing / look | Focal length | Aperture | Camera relationship | Focus / DOF | Composition and resulting look |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| **Epic environmental wide** | 18–24mm | f/8–11 | Distant enough to establish the world | Deep | Environment dominates; character establishes scale; strong geography |
| **Character inside a large environment** | 24–32mm | f/5.6–8 | Fairly close to character | Medium/deep | Character remains important while environment carries story information |
| **Classic cinematic medium-wide** | 28–35mm | f/4–5.6 | Human-scale, moderately close | Medium | Balanced character/environment relationship; dimensional but not distorted |
| **Deep-space mise-en-scène** | 24–32mm | f/8–11 | Close to a meaningful foreground plane | Deep | Deliberately distinct foreground, middle ground and background with story information in each |
| **Immersive / inside the action** | 21–28mm | f/4–5.6 | Physically close to action | Medium/deep | Foreground interaction, strong motion through space, camera feels present |
| **Natural human observation** | 40–50mm | f/4–5.6 | Normal conversational distance | Medium | Restrained perspective; image feels witnessed rather than demonstrated |
| **Character-focused medium shot** | 50–65mm | f/2.8–4 | Moderate distance | Medium/shallow | Character dominates while setting remains identifiable |
| **Intimate close-up** | 75–100mm | f/2–2.8 | Camera backed away | Shallow | Face dominates; environment simplifies; intimate controlled attention |
| **Extreme emotional isolation** | 85–135mm | f/1.4–2 | Distant enough to preserve facial perspective | Very shallow | Eyes/face become the perceptual world; environment dissolves |
| **Three-character dramatic staging** | 28–40mm | f/5.6–8 | Close enough to read expression and body language | Medium/deep | Characters can occupy different depths while their relationships remain readable |
| **Over-the-shoulder dialogue** | 40–65mm | f/2.8–4 | Just behind foreground actor | Medium/shallow | Foreground body establishes relationship; opposing face gets attention |
| **Claustrophobic interior** | 20–28mm | f/2.8–4 | Camera physically inside the constrained space | Medium | Walls and nearby objects press into frame; strong physical presence |
| **Low-angle heroic** | 24–35mm | f/4–8 | Close and below waist height | Medium/deep | Foreground expands; subject gains monumentality and strong silhouette |
| **Threatening / confrontational proximity** | 24–32mm | f/2.8–4 | Uncomfortably close | Medium | Perspective exaggeration makes presence immediate and aggressive |
| **Elegant portrait** | 75–100mm | f/2.8–4 | Backed away | Shallow/medium | Clean facial perspective and controlled separation |
| **Compressed crowd / city / pursuit** | 100–200mm | f/4–5.6 | Far from subjects | Medium | People, vehicles and architecture appear densely stacked along camera axis |
| **Distant observational** | 135–300mm | f/4–8 | Very distant | Medium | Camera feels detached, hidden or unable to intervene |
| **Flat graphic composition** | 85–150mm | f/4–8 | Far back | Medium/deep | Reduced apparent depth; subjects and architecture become graphic layers |
| **Subjective / poetic character** | 50–100mm | f/1.4–2.8 | Position motivated by character perception | Selective | Negative space, partial framing and selective focus privilege emotional attention over geography |
| **Fast readable action** | 24–35mm | f/5.6–8 | Relatively close | Medium/deep | Protagonist, threat and escape route remain connected in one understandable spatial event |

# 3. Modifiers — Change the Feeling Without Rebuilding the Shot

Use these after choosing the basic framing recipe.

| Modifier | Setting | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Focal length** | Go wider while maintaining subject size by moving closer | More foreground scale, stronger near/far relationships, greater physical presence |
|  | Go longer while maintaining subject size by moving farther away | Flatter-looking space, cleaner graphic relationships, greater detachment |
| **Aperture** | Open toward f/1.4–2 | Collapse attention onto one plane; reduce environmental readability |
|  | Close toward f/8–11 | Increase relational storytelling across multiple planes |
| **Camera distance** | Move physically closer | Stronger perspective, intimacy, foreground dominance, visceral movement |
|  | Move physically farther away | Reduced perspective differences, observational distance, apparent compression |
| **Camera height** | 0.3–0.8m | Enlarged foreground, speed, monumentality, vulnerability to passing objects |
|  | 1.2–1.7m | Human-scale participation |
|  | 2–4m | More floor/ground geography, clearer blocking |
|  | Very high | Tactical, omniscient, pattern-oriented view |
| **Camera angle** | Frontal | Direct, graphic, confrontational, architectural |
|  | 3/4 | Dimensional, descriptive, useful for relationships |
|  | Profile | Graphic movement and opposition |
|  | Rear / following | Subjective discovery and audience alignment with character |
| **Focus strategy** | Foreground focus | Immediate object/person becomes priority; deeper action becomes context |
|  | Character focus | Conventional narrative hierarchy |
|  | Background focus | Foreground becomes framing or obstruction |
|  | Deep focus | Audience can inspect several simultaneous story planes |
| **Foreground occupancy** | Increase | Adds depth, occlusion and camera presence |
|  | Reduce | Cleaner, more objective or graphic composition |
| **Negative space** | Increase | Isolation, anticipation, offscreen threat, emotional distance |
|  | Reduce | Pressure, density, immediacy |
| **Shutter angle** | 45–90° | Crisp/staccato action |
|  | 180° | Normal cinematic motion |
|  | 270–360° | Smearing, panic, dream state |
| **Lens distortion** | More corrected | Precision, architecture, formalism |
|  | More wide-angle character | Physicality, edge energy, imperfect immediacy |
| **Lens character** | Modern / high correction | Controlled, precise, clean |
|  | Vintage / lower contrast | Flare, softer falloff, period or romantic optical personality |
| **Camera stability** | Locked | Formality, observation, tension through stillness |
|  | Dolly / track | Spatial revelation and controlled transformation of composition |
|  | Handheld / reactive | Human presence, instability, immediacy — only when dramatically motivated |
| **Plane separation** | Strong FG / MG / BG | Dimensional mise-en-scène and environmental storytelling |
|  | Deliberately collapsed | Graphic, compressed, confrontational or abstract composition |

# 4. Cinematography Prompt Recipe Format

A useful generation prompt should describe **visual behavior first and camera metadata second**. Numbers are there to reinforce the intended image, not substitute for it.

Use this structure:

```text
CAMERA / LENS RECIPE

Framing: [shot scale and subject relationship]
Lens: [focal length] [spherical/anamorphic if important]
Camera distance: [close / moderate / distant + why]
Camera height: [height / relationship to subject]
Aperture: [f-stop]
Focus: [focus plane]
Depth of field: [shallow / medium / deep and what must remain readable]
Shutter: [angle when movement matters]
Composition: [foreground / middle ground / background strategy, negative space, eyelines, geometry]
Optical behavior: [perspective, distortion, compression, falloff]
Result: [plain-language cinematic effect]
```

### Example — Deep-Space Character

```text
CAMERA / LENS RECIPE

Framing: medium-wide three-character ensemble
Lens: 28mm spherical, full-frame equivalent
Camera distance: physically close to a meaningful foreground element
Camera height: approximately 1.3m, human working-deck height
Aperture: f/8
Focus: middle-distance dramatic plane
Depth of field: deep; foreground, middle-ground and background story information remain legible
Shutter: 180 degrees
Composition: three deliberately distinct depth planes with overlapping forms; no flat lineup
Optical behavior: strong but natural near-to-far scale relationships without extreme edge distortion
Result: the audience reads character conflict and the physical space simultaneously; the shot feels capable of sustaining the scene without an immediate cut
```

### Example — Subjective Character

```text
CAMERA / LENS RECIPE

Framing: close or medium-close emphasis on one principal character
Lens: 85mm spherical, full-frame equivalent
Camera distance: backed away enough to preserve natural facial perspective
Camera height: eye level
Aperture: f/2
Focus: principal character's eyes
Depth of field: shallow; secondary characters remain present but substantially softer
Shutter: 180 degrees
Composition: emotionally motivated negative space and partial framing; geography is subordinate to perception
Optical behavior: narrow field of view and selective focus simplify the world around the character
Result: the frame expresses what the character is attending to rather than objectively cataloguing the scene
```

These recipes should remain subordinate to the **cinematography grammar**. If a numeric choice conflicts with the intended visual behavior, preserve the visual behavior and adjust the number.
