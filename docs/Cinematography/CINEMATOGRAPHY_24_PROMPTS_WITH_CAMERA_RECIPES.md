# 01. Classical Adventure — Camera as Storyteller

## 01-A — Discovery — Ragamuffin / Spaceport

```text
OUTPUT CONTRACT

Generate exactly ONE image representing ONE continuous movie shot.

Aspect ratio: 16:9.

The image fills the entire canvas.

No typography.
No labels.
No borders.
No split screen.
No panels.
No storyboard.
No contact sheet.
No mood board.
No poster design.
No multiple moments.
No inset images.

CALIBRATION CONTINUITY

This is a controlled cinematography test. The story event, principal character identities, costumes, core props, vehicles, and basic environment are locked across cinematography styles.

The cinematography style may change camera position, lens behavior, framing, blocking emphasis, depth, lighting, exposure, color strategy, focus, camera-subject relationship, and visual rhythm.

Do not redesign the story, principal characters, costumes, vehicles, creatures, props, or world to express the cinematography style.

SCENE — DISCOVERY

A scruffy young ragamuffin walks through a dusty desert city and spaceport in a heavily used science-fiction world.

It is daytime. The city is crowded and alive with humans and unfamiliar alien life. Practical hover speeders move through the streets. Spacecraft depart in the far distance.

The protagonist is experiencing this place and its scale. The image should let the audience discover the world with the character rather than merely presenting an empty establishing shot.

The frame must communicate discovery, readable geography, world scale, foreground/middle-ground/background relationships, environmental storytelling, and the protagonist's relationship to the city.

CHARACTER CONTINUITY

The ragamuffin is the same protagonist in every cinematography-style rendering of this scene. Preserve face, apparent age, hair, build, costume, and core equipment. Do not invent a new protagonist for another style.

CINEMATOGRAPHY GRAMMAR

CLASSICAL ADVENTURE CINEMATOGRAPHY — CAMERA AS STORYTELLER

Stage the image for immediate narrative clarity, discovery, scale, and adventure.

Use strong foreground, middle-ground, and background relationships. Build depth into the composition. Establish clear geography and readable character silhouettes.

Choose a purposeful cinematic camera position rather than generic coverage. The frame should suggest that camera movement, character movement, or a reveal could naturally transform this composition into another meaningful composition.

Favor moderate wide-angle cinematic perspective, strong eyelines, motivated practical and natural lighting, dimensional atmosphere, and clear separation of subjects.

The image should feel exciting without becoming chaotic, elegant without becoming formalistic, and spectacular without sacrificing spatial clarity.

Prioritize:
story information
visual discovery
readable geography
character relationships
scale
silhouette
depth
clear visual hierarchy

Avoid:
generic coverage
arbitrary Dutch angles
excessive shallow depth of field
unmotivated handheld photography
flat staging
fashion photography
symmetry for its own sake
visual complexity that obscures the story

CAMERA / LENS RECIPE

Framing: medium-wide discovery composition with protagonist integrated into environment
Lens: 32mm spherical, full-frame equivalent
Camera distance: moderately close to protagonist while retaining a broad view of the spaceport
Camera height: approximately 1.4m, human-scale
Aperture: f/5.6
Focus: protagonist / middle-distance activity
Depth of field: medium-deep; foreground, protagonist and major background geography remain readable
Shutter: 180 degrees
Composition: purposeful foreground, middle-ground and background relationships; clear route of visual discovery
Optical behavior: dimensional moderate-wide perspective without conspicuous distortion
Result: the audience discovers scale and story information with the character while geography stays immediately understandable
```

SCENE — CHARACTER

Three men are aboard a small, old, hard-used fishing boat at sea.

The dramatic center is the relationship between the men, NOT the shark.

The protagonist and the older boat captain are in a tense face-to-face disagreement in the working area of the boat. They are close enough that their expressions and body language can be clearly read. The third crewman is nearby and reacts to the conflict, creating a three-person dramatic relationship rather than an isolated portrait or generic two-shot.

The disagreement is about whether they should keep pursuing a dangerous great white shark or turn back.

Their eyelines, distance, blocking, posture, expressions, and reactions must communicate conflicting intentions even without dialogue.

Evidence of the shark hunt exists around them: wet working deck, heavy fishing line, damaged or stressed fishing equipment, and other practical signs of the hunt. The shark itself must NOT dominate the frame. If visible at all, it is secondary story information.

The frame must communicate character relationships, blocking, eyelines, power, emotional tension, reaction, intimacy versus environment, and visual storytelling through human behavior.
