# The Beltminers — Cinematography Styles

## Purpose

These eight cinematography styles are reusable visual grammars for film production stills, scene development, and cinematography planning.

Each style uses a common format:

1. Key Question
2. Description
3. Operating Principle
4. Visual Mechanics
5. Image-Model Prompt
6. Five Reference Films

The styles describe camera behavior, lighting, composition, depth, movement, and visual storytelling—not genre.

---

# 1. Classical Adventure — Camera as Storyteller

## Key Question

How can the camera tell the story most clearly and excitingly?

## Description

Bold, lucid visual storytelling in which composition, blocking, camera placement, and movement reveal information with clarity and momentum. Geography remains understandable, spectacle has scale, and the camera often discovers something with the audience. Frames feel cinematic without becoming self-conscious.

## Operating Principle

Every camera position, move, and cut should reveal story information or change our emotional relationship to it.

## Visual Mechanics

- Strong foreground / middle-ground / background staging
- Moderate wides mixed with purposeful close-ups
- Clearly readable silhouettes
- Motivated dollies, pans, push-ins, and reveals
- Characters frequently lead camera movement
- Strong eyelines and screen direction
- Geography established before action disrupts it
- Cinematic but motivated lighting
- Favor evolving compositions over arbitrary coverage


## Camera Recipes

Camera as storyteller means the frame stays legible: human-scale distances, balanced character-and-environment, nothing the audience must decode. Framings from `docs/CAMERA_RECIPES.md`; the first is the
default when a panel chooses nothing.

- `classic-cinematic-medium-wide` — the default register — dimensional without distortion
- `epic-environmental-wide` — when the world is the story beat
- `character-focused-medium-shot` — when one person carries the moment
- `three-character-dramatic-staging` — when the relationships are the subject
- `over-the-shoulder-dialogue` — exchanges
- `low-angle-heroic` — when a subject must gain stature
- `fast-readable-action` — action that must stay understandable
## Image-Model Prompt

```text
CLASSICAL ADVENTURE CINEMATOGRAPHY — CAMERA AS STORYTELLER

Stage the image for immediate narrative clarity, discovery, scale, and adventure.

Use strong foreground, middle-ground, and background relationships. Build depth into the composition. Establish clear geography and readable character silhouettes.

Choose a purposeful cinematic camera position rather than generic coverage. The frame should suggest that camera movement, character movement, or a reveal could naturally transform this composition into another meaningful composition.

Favor moderate wide-angle cinematic perspective, strong eyelines, motivated practical and natural lighting, dimensional atmosphere, and clear separation of subjects.

The image should feel exciting without becoming chaotic, elegant without becoming formalistic, and spectacular without sacrificing spatial clarity.

Prioritize:
story information
visual discovery
readable geography
character relationships
scale
silhouette
depth
clear visual hierarchy

Avoid:
generic coverage
arbitrary Dutch angles
excessive shallow depth of field
unmotivated handheld photography
flat staging
fashion photography
symmetry for its own sake
visual complexity that obscures the story
```

## Reference Films

- Raiders of the Lost Ark
- Jaws
- Star Wars
- Back to the Future
- Jurassic Park

# 2. Deep-Space Mise-en-Scène — World Inside the Frame

## Key Question

Can blocking and depth tell the story inside one frame?

## Description

The frame contains multiple layers of meaningful action. Foreground, middle ground, and background remain dramatically connected, allowing relationships, power, and story information to coexist rather than being broken into conventional coverage.

## Operating Principle

Stage relationships in depth before solving them with editing.

## Visual Mechanics

- Wide or moderate-wide lenses
- Deep focus or generous depth of field
- Strong near-camera foreground objects or characters
- Multiple planes of action
- Architectural framing
- Characters differentiated through scale and position
- Longer compositions capable of sustaining blocking


## Camera Recipes

The whole grammar is about information in every plane, so the framings all deepen the frame rather than flattening it. Framings from `docs/CAMERA_RECIPES.md`; the first is the
default when a panel chooses nothing.

- `deep-space-mise-en-scene` — the grammar's own row — distinct FG/MG/BG, each carrying story
- `character-inside-a-large-environment` — when the environment outweighs the person
- `epic-environmental-wide` — establishing the world's scale
- `three-character-dramatic-staging` — people at different depths, all readable
- `claustrophobic-interior` — the same depth logic inside a small volume
## Image-Model Prompt

```text
DEEP-SPACE MISE-EN-SCÈNE CINEMATOGRAPHY

Construct the frame in meaningful foreground, middle-ground, and background layers.

Keep spatial relationships readable across the image. Use generous depth of field rather than isolating one subject against an abstract blurred background.

Place important story information at different distances from camera. Use architecture, doorways, windows, machinery, landscape, or foreground characters to create dimensional staging.

Favor a wide or moderately wide cinematic perspective with strong near-to-far scale relationships.

The composition should feel capable of sustaining an entire dramatic scene without immediately requiring a cut.

Prioritize:
depth
blocking
spatial relationships
environmental storytelling
foreground scale
multiple planes of meaningful action

Avoid:
flat lineups
empty foregrounds
telephoto compression
extreme background blur
single-subject portrait composition
meaningless environmental clutter
```

## Reference Films

- Citizen Kane
- The Best Years of Our Lives
- Seven Samurai
- High and Low
- There Will Be Blood

# 3. Expressionist / Noir — Light as Psychology

## Key Question

What does the scene feel like psychologically?

## Description

Lighting and composition express psychological or moral conditions rather than merely describing physical space. Darkness can hide information, architecture can threaten characters, and faces may be partially withheld.

## Operating Principle

Light what matters emotionally; allow everything else to disappear.

## Visual Mechanics

- Deep blacks
- Selective illumination
- Hard directional sources where appropriate
- Negative space
- Silhouettes
- Faces crossing light and shadow
- Strong geometry
- Controlled highlights
- Intentional withholding of visual information


## Camera Recipes

Light is the psychology, and these framings put a subject where light can do something to them — pressed, isolated, or looming. Framings from `docs/CAMERA_RECIPES.md`; the first is the
default when a panel chooses nothing.

- `claustrophobic-interior` — the default — walls and objects press in and take the light
- `low-angle-heroic` — silhouette and monumentality
- `intimate-close-up` — the face as the lit surface
- `extreme-emotional-isolation` — when the environment should dissolve entirely
- `threatening-confrontational-proximity` — presence made aggressive
- `flat-graphic-composition` — when light should read as shape rather than volume
## Image-Model Prompt

```text
EXPRESSIONIST / NOIR CINEMATOGRAPHY — LIGHT AS PSYCHOLOGY

Treat darkness, light, and negative space as dramatic elements.

Do not illuminate the entire environment evenly. Selectively reveal the information that matters and allow portions of faces, architecture, and background to disappear into controlled darkness.

Use directional motivated light, strong value separation, silhouettes, architectural shadows, and carefully controlled highlights.

Composition may feel psychologically oppressive, secretive, or morally uncertain.

Prioritize:
chiaroscuro
negative space
silhouette
psychological framing
selective visibility
graphic light and shadow

Avoid:
flat fill light
bright ambient exposure everywhere
beauty lighting
gratuitous neon
random colored lighting
texture visible merely because it exists
```

## Reference Films

- The Third Man
- The Night of the Hunter
- The Godfather
- Blade Runner
- Se7en

# 4. Naturalistic / Observational — Discovered Light

## Key Question

What if we let the world provide the image?

## Description

Photography appears discovered rather than imposed. Sun, windows, fire, practical lamps, overcast skies, and twilight determine the image. Camera placement tends toward observation rather than proclamation.

## Operating Principle

Find the beautiful image already present in the world before adding anything to it.

## Visual Mechanics

- Available or motivated light
- Backlight
- Window light
- Firelight
- Natural exposure falloff
- Restrained fill
- Environmental color
- Imperfect but believable illumination
- Observational camera placement


## Camera Recipes

Discovered light asks the camera to witness rather than demonstrate, so the framings are the restrained ones — normal distances, unexceptional angles. Framings from `docs/CAMERA_RECIPES.md`; the first is the
default when a panel chooses nothing.

- `natural-human-observation` — the default — restrained perspective, image feels witnessed
- `distant-observational` — when the camera should feel unable to intervene
- `character-focused-medium-shot` — one person, setting still identifiable
- `character-inside-a-large-environment` — a person inside a place that continues without them
- `over-the-shoulder-dialogue` — exchanges without staging them
## Image-Model Prompt

```text
NATURALISTIC OBSERVATIONAL CINEMATOGRAPHY

Make the image feel discovered in a real environment rather than artificially lit for a movie.

Base illumination on believable natural or practical sources: sun, sky, windows, fire, lamps, or existing environmental light.

Allow realistic falloff into shadow. Do not fill every face perfectly. Preserve believable backlight, imperfect exposure relationships, and natural variation across the environment.

Use restrained camera placement and composition that feels observant rather than demonstrative.

Prioritize:
natural light
motivated practical sources
environmental authenticity
subtle exposure
real atmosphere
unforced composition

Avoid:
obvious studio lighting
perfect rim lights
excessive fill
gratuitous haze
beauty lighting
hyper-polished HDR
cinematic lighting without a believable source
```

## Reference Films

- Days of Heaven
- Barry Lyndon
- Paris, Texas
- No Country for Old Men
- Nomadland

# 5. Formalist / Architectural — Frame as System

## Key Question

What visual system governs this world?

## Description

Composition is visibly designed. Symmetry, frontal staging, architecture, repeated geometry, and precise spatial relationships make the audience aware of the frame as an organizing system. This can feel imposing and inhuman or playful and storybook-like.

## Operating Principle

Establish a visual rule and make everything in the frame obey it.

## Visual Mechanics

- One-point perspective
- Symmetry or deliberately controlled asymmetry
- Frontal characters
- Perpendicular camera relationships
- Architectural axes
- Repeated geometry
- Precise headroom
- Controlled palette
- Camera position determined by composition


## Camera Recipes

The frame is a system, so the framings are the ones that flatten space into deliberate graphic relationships. Framings from `docs/CAMERA_RECIPES.md`; the first is the
default when a panel chooses nothing.

- `flat-graphic-composition` — the default — subjects and architecture become layers
- `epic-environmental-wide` — geometry at the scale of the world
- `distant-observational` — detachment as a formal choice
- `elegant-portrait` — controlled separation, clean perspective
- `deep-space-mise-en-scene` — when the system is depth rather than plane
## Image-Model Prompt

```text
FORMALIST ARCHITECTURAL CINEMATOGRAPHY

Treat the frame as a deliberately constructed visual system.

Use precise camera placement, strong architectural geometry, controlled perspective, and intentional spatial relationships.

Favor frontal staging, one-point perspective, symmetry, or rigorously balanced asymmetry. Align characters and objects deliberately with the architecture.

Every line, shape, and placement should feel intentional.

Prioritize:
geometry
symmetry
architectural framing
precision
repetition
controlled palette
graphic organization

Avoid:
casual framing
accidental tangents
documentary looseness
random camera height
arbitrary handheld movement
messy visual hierarchy
uncontrolled background elements
```

## Reference Films

- 2001: A Space Odyssey
- The Shining
- Playtime
- The Grand Budapest Hotel
- The French Dispatch

# 6. Immersive / Experiential — Camera Inside the Event

## Key Question

What does it feel like to physically be there?

## Description

The camera shares physical space and duration with the characters. Instead of presenting an idealized view of action, it experiences movement, obstruction, danger, and discovery alongside them.

## Operating Principle

Place the audience physically inside the event rather than safely outside observing it.

## Visual Mechanics

- Human-height camera
- Close spatial relationships
- Foreground occlusion
- Characters passing near lens
- Dynamic reframing
- Long developing compositions
- Environmental motion
- Physical lens proximity
- Camera movement motivated by following, avoiding, searching, or discovering


## Camera Recipes

The camera is inside the event, which means physically close and wide — every row here has the camera in the space rather than observing it. Framings from `docs/CAMERA_RECIPES.md`; the first is the
default when a panel chooses nothing.

- `immersive-inside-the-action` — the default — foreground interaction, camera present
- `fast-readable-action` — when the event must still parse
- `claustrophobic-interior` — inside a constrained volume
- `threatening-confrontational-proximity` — when proximity is the threat
- `low-angle-heroic` — the body from below, foreground expanded
## Image-Model Prompt

```text
IMMERSIVE EXPERIENTIAL CINEMATOGRAPHY

Place the camera physically inside the event with the characters.

Use human-scale camera placement and strong physical proximity. Allow foreground objects, people, or environmental elements to partially obstruct the frame when appropriate.

Create the feeling that the camera must react to unfolding events rather than observing them from a perfect external position.

Favor compositions suggesting continuous movement, following, searching, avoidance, or discovery.

Prioritize:
physical presence
proximity
movement
foreground interaction
environmental immersion
subjective geography

Avoid:
omniscient overhead coverage
perfectly staged action tableau
telephoto observation from safety
gratuitous shaky camera
music-video angles
action without readable spatial relationships
```

## Reference Films

- The Battle of Algiers
- Goodfellas
- Saving Private Ryan
- Children of Men
- 1917

# 7. Chromatic / Operatic — Color as Drama

## Key Question

What can color and light say that dialogue cannot?

## Description

Color is dramatic structure rather than decoration. Lighting, costume, environment, and production design cooperate around deliberate color relationships. Reality can bend toward emotion while remaining photographically coherent.

## Operating Principle

Assign dramatic meaning to color, then control the frame around that relationship.

## Visual Mechanics

- Dominant and subordinate hues
- Warm / cool relationships
- Controlled saturation
- Silhouette against color fields
- Colored practical sources where motivated
- Atmosphere carrying light
- Costume and environment deliberately separated or harmonized


## Camera Recipes

This grammar constrains COLOUR, not framing, and its list is deliberately wide for that reason. Where a shot needs colour to read as a block, the graphic and portrait rows serve it best; otherwise choose on the shot. Framings from `docs/CAMERA_RECIPES.md`; the first is the
default when a panel chooses nothing.

- `classic-cinematic-medium-wide` — the default — colour carries the drama, the framing stays open
- `flat-graphic-composition` — when colour should read as flat, adjacent areas
- `elegant-portrait` — a face as the colour event
- `epic-environmental-wide` — colour at the scale of a place
- `intimate-close-up` — colour on skin and near surfaces
- `extreme-emotional-isolation` — when everything but the colour of one face should go
## Image-Model Prompt

```text
CHROMATIC OPERATIC CINEMATOGRAPHY — COLOR AS DRAMA

Build the image around a deliberate dramatic color relationship.

Choose a dominant color family and a controlled secondary or opposing color. Coordinate lighting, environment, costume, and atmosphere around that relationship.

Push saturation hard where colour carries meaning, and let the rest fall away. Allow colour to express emotional state, danger, intimacy, power, or transformation.

Maintain strong value structure beneath the color design.

Prioritize:
intentional palette
color contrast
emotional color
silhouette
decisive saturation
light interacting with atmosphere

Avoid:
rainbow palettes
random colored lights
generic teal-and-orange grading
neon simply to appear cinematic
equal saturation everywhere
color without dramatic purpose
```

## Reference Films

- The Red Shoes
- The Conformist
- Apocalypse Now
- In the Mood for Love
- Hero

# 8. Subjective / Poetic — Camera as Consciousness

## Key Question

What does this moment feel like inside the character?

## Description

Physical reality becomes subordinate to perception. Framing, focus, movement, exposure, reflection, and visual rhythm communicate memory, desire, fear, isolation, or altered perception.

## Operating Principle

Allow perception and emotion to distort objective photographic reality.

## Visual Mechanics

- Selective focus
- Fragmented framing
- Reflections
- Negative space
- Faces partially obscured
- Unusual subject placement
- Visual repetition
- Motion blur when meaningful
- Shifting focus
- Environmental abstraction
- Camera distance driven by emotional state


## Camera Recipes

Camera as consciousness is the grammar that suffered most from being written in adjectives: `selective focus`, `negative space` and `unusual placement` are all satisfied by an everything-sharp frame. These rows are not. Framings from `docs/CAMERA_RECIPES.md`; the first is the
default when a panel chooses nothing.

- `subjective-poetic-character` — the grammar's own row — 50–100mm at f/1.4–2.8, selective
- `extreme-emotional-isolation` — 85–135mm at f/1.4–2; the environment dissolves
- `intimate-close-up` — attention held on one face
- `immersive-inside-the-action` — perception from inside the moment
- `claustrophobic-interior` — perception pressed by the space
## Image-Model Prompt

```text
SUBJECTIVE POETIC CINEMATOGRAPHY — CAMERA AS CONSCIOUSNESS

Photograph the scene according to the character's internal experience rather than objective spatial description.

Use selective focus, negative space, reflections, partial framing, environmental abstraction and unusual subject placement where the moment carries feeling.

Allow memory, loneliness, fear, desire, or disorientation to influence composition, focus, exposure, and visual rhythm.

The image should remain intentional and emotionally legible rather than becoming randomly surreal.

Prioritize:
point of view
emotion
perception
selective attention
negative space
visual metaphor
subjective focus

Avoid:
arbitrary surrealism
random lens effects
visual distortion without emotional purpose
generic dreamy glow
effects for decoration
objective establishing-shot composition
```

## Reference Films

- Persona
- Mirror
- Taxi Driver
- Chungking Express
- Aftersun

## Usage Note

For image generation, the reference film titles should remain human-facing context. The actual generation prompt should rely on the visual mechanics and operating principle rather than asking the image model to imitate a specific film.

These grammars can later be combined as weighted cinematography systems—for example:

- 70% Classical Adventure
- 20% Naturalistic / Observational
- 10% Expressionist / Noir

The weighting should influence mechanics such as framing, lens behavior, lighting, depth, movement, and color rather than averaging the aesthetics of the reference films.
