# Scene Extraction Protocol

## Goal

Convert a screenplay sequence into a reliable visual brief before image generation.

---

## Step 1 — Locate the Correct Sequence

Search using several anchors, not one:

- location
- vehicle
- character names
- distinctive dialogue
- final action
- transition after the scene

For the Genetics escape sequence, useful anchors include:

- Genetics
- GT40
- Jake
- bridge
- handle
- "Punch it"
- blue flash
- Resistance base arrival

---

## Step 2 — Read the Full Dramatic Unit

Read:

- the setup immediately before the chase
- the entire chase
- the bridge jump
- the disappearance
- the arrival beat afterward

Do not isolate a single page if the emotional tone depends on previous pages.

---

## Step 3 — Produce a Literal Beat Sheet

Write only what the screenplay establishes.

Example format:

```text
01. Charlie and Jake leave the Genetics in the GT40.
02. GRM dune buggies pursue them.
03. A GRM hover jet closes from above.
04. A Genetic is attached to the hover jet.
05. The chase enters a canyon.
06. The bridge ahead is destroyed.
07. Charlie opens a hidden panel.
08. A handwritten warning tells Jake not to touch the handle.
09. Charlie tells Jake to accelerate and make the car fly.
10. The Genetic tears the hover jet's aileron away.
11. The hover jet spirals into the canyon wall.
12. The GT40 leaves the broken bridge.
13. Charlie pulls the handle.
14. A blue flash consumes the car and nearby wreckage.
15. The vehicles and debris vanish.
16. Silence and wind.
17. Later, a blue rift opens above the Resistance base.
18. The GT40 falls out and crashes to a stop.
```

The exact beat sheet must be verified against the current screenplay before production.

---

## Step 4 — Extract Visual Facts

Create five groups.

### People

- characters present
- seating positions
- actions
- injuries
- wardrobe stated or previously locked
- emotional beat

### Vehicles and Props

- exact model
- exact variant
- count
- damage
- orientation
- attachments
- hidden mechanisms
- labels

### Environment

- terrain
- road
- bridge
- canyon width
- settlement geometry
- time of day
- atmosphere
- visibility

### Effects

- weapons fire
- smoke
- fire
- debris
- blue warp behavior
- disappearance
- rift behavior
- arrival impact

### Tone

- trust between Charlie and Jake
- dangerous momentum
- humor under pressure
- impossible leap
- sudden silence
- triumphant but rough arrival

---

## Step 5 — List Forbidden Additions

Examples:

- Kyra in the GT40
- a convoy larger than scripted
- canyon dogfight not in the scene
- missiles if not present in the current draft
- generic portal ring
- extra Onyx Units
- extra Genetics
- slow-motion hero poses
- a pristine racetrack GT40
- futuristic digital cockpit replacing the approved car interior

---

## Step 6 — User Confirmation Gate

Before creating a first board for a newly identified sequence, provide:

- one-paragraph scene summary
- literal beat sheet
- characters present
- key vehicles
- final visual beat

Generation begins only after the scene identity is confirmed, unless the user explicitly says to proceed without confirmation.

---

## Step 7 — Convert to Visual Opportunities

Not every beat becomes a panel.

Select a small number of images that define production design:

- one hero action image
- one interior character/mechanism image
- one vehicle-threat image
- one environment or bridge image
- one effect study
- unique materials and construction references

A scene board is not a storyboard.
