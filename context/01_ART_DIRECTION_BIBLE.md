# The Beltminers — Locked Art Direction Bible

## Status

This document is the authoritative visual context for all master and scene art-direction boards.

Agents must not reinterpret, modernize, embellish, or replace these rules without explicit user instruction.

---

## Overall Visual Identity

The Beltminers is grounded science fiction with the feeling of:

- ILM production development boards
- Ralph McQuarrie production paintings
- *Andor*
- *Rogue One*
- *The Empire Strikes Back*
- Apollo-era NASA
- practical mining and aviation hardware
- worn, maintained, human-built environments
- painterly concept art with visible brushwork

The world must feel designed for production, not designed for marketing.

---

## Rendering Language

### Required

- Painterly production concept art
- Brushwork supports large readable forms — do not simulate brush texture
  across every surface; favor shape readability over surface texture
- Large value grouping over local detail
- Strong silhouette design
- Cinematic value grouping
- Practical material logic
- Materials read at production-board viewing distance: value, edge quality,
  and color before texture — no microscopic surface variation
- Real-world construction logic
- Restrained visual effects
- Clear production-design intent
- Board layouts that resemble an internal studio development wall

### Avoid

- Glossy promotional key art
- Photobash collage appearance
- Over-rendered surfaces
- Anime influence
- Fantasy costuming
- Decorative complexity without function
- Generic futuristic interfaces
- Repeated three-quarter views
- Repeated medium-grey panels
- Excessive visual noise
- Dense repetitive micro-texture: dot fields, repeated curved strokes,
  embossed or fingerprint-like patterning
- Giant portal rings unless explicitly present in the screenplay

### Texture budget

Texture exists only to communicate material, scale, wear, or manufacture.

- Every object reads as an object first; texture supports form and must
  never become the subject.
- Do not texture empty surfaces simply to increase visual richness.
- If texture competes with silhouette, remove texture. If texture obscures
  form, simplify.

## Technology Families

## Resistance

Keywords: resistance, cabin, shack, workshop, homestead, mine, mining, miner, camp, pioneer, charlie, settlement

**Design language:** handmade, repairable, practical, lived-in.

- welded assemblies
- mining equipment
- aviation surplus
- repurposed pre-Fall machinery
- analog controls where appropriate
- visible fasteners
- replaceable modules
- repaired surfaces
- dirt from use
- maintained rather than abandoned
- little meaningless trash
- tools and materials stored with purpose

The Resistance is not a junkyard culture. Their equipment is old because they preserve it.

---

## GRM

**Design language:** precise, sterile, authoritarian, controlled.

- dark alloys
- minimal seams
- exact geometry
- repeated manufacturing standards
- cold lighting
- hidden service access
- clean interfaces
- perfect alignment
- visual hierarchy that communicates control
- intimidation through order, not ornament

---

## Beltminer Technology

**Design language:** ancient, elegant, powerful, and difficult to fully understand.

- impossible engineering
- restrained blue-white energy
- localized light bending
- controlled spatial distortion
- highly purposeful forms
- no decorative complexity
- no magical runes
- no oversized portal architecture unless specifically scripted
- technology should feel revered, protected, and dangerous

Beltminer effects should feel sacred without becoming mystical fantasy.

## Lighting Language

Use strong contrast between:

- warm human light
- cold GRM light
- blue Beltminer energy
- natural exterior light

Approved atmosphere studies include:

- golden meadow light
- clean morning
- dusk and lanterns
- storm approach
- cold GRM white light
- Onyx red optics
- restrained Beltminer blue glow
- asteroid shadows

Lighting should define faction, emotion, and function.

## Composition Rules

A board must read clearly from twenty feet away.

Each major panel should have a distinct visual role:

- hero environment
- character relationship
- vehicle silhouette
- prop or mechanism
- materials
- lighting
- effect behavior
- location geometry
- scale reference

Avoid filling a board with several variations of the same image.

No more than one dominant hero image of the same beat unless the user explicitly requests alternatives.

---

## Character Presentation

Characters must be grounded in screenplay description and approved likeness references.

Do not invent:

- costumes
- weapons
- accessories
- hairstyles
- body types
- emotional states
- scene presence

A character appearing elsewhere in the story is not permission to place them in a scene.

---

## Production Board Presentation

Preferred board characteristics:

- wide cinematic canvas
- warm beige or production-paper ground when appropriate
- clear editorial hierarchy
- large hero image
- a small number of supporting scene shots
- unique reference cells
- materials and texture strip
- lighting studies
- effect studies
- concise production notes
- legible labels
- no redundant reference rows

## Current Locked Scene-Specific Lessons

### Charlie's Workshop

Keywords: charlie, workshop, cabin, shack, smelter, stove

- rustic but clean
- compact
- organized
- maintained
- very little trash
- dawn or pre-dawn contrast when required
- exterior view used when showing light escaping through wall cracks
- covered yellow GT40 Mk II when the screenplay says it is covered
- Beltminer device should be simple, compact, rounded or oval when approved
- device detail should remain closer to an old appliance or espresso-machine level of complexity than a dense sci-fi engine
- digital display is acceptable
- avoid unnecessary analog gauges

### GT40

Keywords: gt40, gt 40, ford, race car, chassis, engine compartment

- true Ford GT40 Mk II
- yellow
- exact rear geometry matters
- twin raised Mk II rear intake forms must affect the tarp silhouette
- preserve orientation and camera angle unless the user requests a change
- do not create extra vehicle reference sections unless asked

## Drift Prevention Rule

Before any prompt is finalized, the Art Direction Guardian must answer:

1. Which locked rules apply?
2. Which approved reference images apply?
3. Which visual choices are prohibited?
4. Which details are scene-specific rather than global?
5. What must remain unchanged from the previous approved board?

If any answer is unknown, generation stops until the source is checked.
