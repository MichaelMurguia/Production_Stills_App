# STORE — STORE_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md

## S1 — The operator console — RATIFY THE PAGE, CORRECT THE LINK

**The console keeps the store's shell and drops the store's salesmanship.**
Header, footer and type scale stay — it is the same site, one nav, one
account session, and inventing a second chrome for one page is a maintenance
tax with no reader benefit. But the sales furniture inside the page does not
belong: no hero rhythm, no trait lists, no provenance lines, no marquee. The
body is a Courier console — facts line, state tables, buttons. That is
already most of what shipped; the ruling is that it must **stay** that way as
the console grows.

Canon (new subsection, `### 9. Internal surfaces`):

> An owner-only page wears the store's shell and none of its persuasion. Sales
> rhythm is for buyers; an internal page states and acts. One fill maximum,
> on the page's single consequential act.

`Mint` keeps the page's one fill. The two state tables keep one-word Courier
states in status colors, never amber — ratified, and it is the correct read
of §8: a state the software enforces is a status color, not the reader's
index.

**The `ADMIN` link is corrected: Courier, `--ink-dim`, not amber.**

It is not a fifth role and must not become one. §8's four roles all answer
"where should the reader look next" — and an owner already knows they are an
owner. The link states a fact about the session, and this system's grammar for
a stated fact is dim Courier. Coining a *role marker* role would also be the
first amber whose meaning is "who you are" rather than "what to do", which is
exactly how a four-role budget becomes six. Keep the link left of the avatar,
keep it owner-only, drop the amber.

Facts-line density as built: ratified.

## S2 — The trial surfaces — RATIFY THE DATE PAIR, DEMOTE THE CODE BOX

**The date pair is ratified as canon.** `TRIAL — N DAYS LEFT` with a Courier
line stating the date and the consequence separately is exactly right, and
the two variants must stay distinct — a trial that will charge a card and a
trial that will stop serving are different futures, and a single "ends"
sentence would flatten them. Canon (§5, gates are stated):

> A dated future state names the date and the consequence as two facts.
> "Ends" alone is not a consequence.

**The two-door page becomes one door and an escape hatch.** Two boxes of
equal weight ask the visitor to choose between paths, and one of those paths
— redemption — is only available to someone who already knows they have a
code. Presenting it as a peer plants the question *"do I need a code?"* in
everyone who doesn't. Correction: the card-trial box keeps the page's single
fill and full §4 trait list; the code box collapses to one line beneath it —
`Have a code? Redeem it` — expanding in place on click. Same function, no
false fork. A running trial still replaces both, unchanged.

Entry points ratified as built: the `Try free` header link, and the Courier
line on the cloud edition card rather than a third fill. Correct — a second
fill in the pricing row would break §8's cap and put two amber buttons side
by side.

**Business is not trialable, and the card must say so.** A trial de-risks a
decision one person makes alone; a Business purchase is made for other people
and usually by someone who is not the evaluator, so a self-serve clock is the
wrong instrument. Silence reads as an oversight, though — add one Courier
line to the Business card stating the position in the store's own vocabulary.
Do not invent a sales motion (no "contact us" form) unless one exists.

## S3 — Studio naming terms + Claim/Rename — RATIFY, ONE CORRECTION

Copy and placement are right: the button naming the act it will perform
(`Claim name` while the slug is auto-assigned, `Rename` after) is the
verb-states-its-act rule working, and the faint terms line belongs under the
form where the consequence lands.

One correction: the terms line states what a rename does **and does not** do.
Keep both halves, but lead with the consequence that costs something. A user
skimming a faint line reads the first clause only, and the clause that
matters is the one that can surprise them later.
